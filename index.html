Ch. 1Diagnosis

What you notice, and what's usually underneath it.

Most of the organizations I talk with describe the same few symptoms. They feel like separate problems. They usually share a root.

Symptom: what you noticeWhat's actually happening
1.a"Every push is tied to an event, a deadline, or a crisis. Then it goes quiet." Reactive instead of strategic.There's no year-round plan, so the calendar decides what gets asked for, and when. Fundraising happens in bursts, and so does burnout.
1.b"People give once, maybe twice, and then we lose track of them." Donor relationships stall.Nobody has mapped what should happen after a first gift, so commitment never gets a clear path to deepen.
1.c"Our executive director is also our development department." No senior fundraising voice.A full-time development director is out of reach, so strategy gets squeezed in between everything else.
1.d"The board is enthusiastic. Nothing really moves." Board energy without direction.Members want to help but haven't been given clear expectations, the right tools, or a defined role in the plan.

Ch. 6Common questions

Questions people ask before we start.

What is a fractional development director?

A fractional development director is a senior fundraising leader who works with your organization part-time. They set priorities, guide strategy across individual, mid-level, and major gifts, and build the systems your team needs, without the cost of a full-time hire. It's built for organizations that need senior fundraising leadership but can't yet justify a full-time development director.

How is fractional development different from a one-time consulting project?

Fractional development is an ongoing partnership: I work alongside your team week to week and adjust as your organization grows. A project, like the Donor Strategy Sprint, has a fixed scope, a fixed price, and a clear end date. Many organizations start with a project and move to fractional support later.

What is an associate board, and how is it different from a board of directors?

A board of directors governs the organization and carries legal and fiduciary responsibility. An associate board is a separate group of committed supporters, often emerging leaders, who raise money, advocate, and expand your network without governance duties. A well-designed associate board can also become a pipeline for future board members and major donors.

How much does it cost to work with GivingBridge?

There are two fixed-price ways to start: the Fundraising Health Check for $79, and the Donor Strategy Sprint for $1,200 flat. Fractional Development and Associate Board Development are scoped to your organization's needs, and we work out the details on a free 15-minute call.

What size organizations do you work with?

Mostly small nonprofits where fundraising rests on a few people: an executive director doing development on the side, a development team of one to three, or a board that wants to help. My own fundraising experience spans organizations with budgets from $700,000 to $450 million.

Do you work with nonprofits outside of New York?

Yes. The Health Check and the Donor Strategy Sprint are done entirely remotely, so your location doesn't matter. For ongoing work, we'll talk through what makes sense on the first call.

Do you write grants or plan events?

No. My focus is fundraising strategy and the systems behind it: donor relationships, board fundraising, and a steady rhythm of engagement. Those are what make grants, events, and campaigns more effective.
